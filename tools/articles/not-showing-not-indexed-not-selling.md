You type your main search term into Amazon, scroll, and your product is not there. Page five, page seven. Nothing. Your campaign is running and your listing is live.

The usual next step is to raise the bid, because the bid is the easiest lever to find. Often that changes nothing, because the bid was never the problem.

## Why can't I find my product when I search Amazon?

Because "I cannot find my product" covers four separate problems that produce the same empty screen.

1. **The ad is not being served.** Amazon shows it to nobody, so impressions — the count of times your ad was displayed — are zero.
2. **The ad is served, but you did not see it.** Amazon ran an auction for that shopper and you did not win it.
3. **The listing is not indexed for that term.** Amazon does not treat it as a candidate, so it cannot appear in the organic (unpaid) results at any position.
4. **The listing is indexed but ranks too low to find.** It is a candidate, simply behind too many other products.

[[The confusion]]
Problems 1 and 2 are advertising. Problems 3 and 4 are organic search. The expensive mistake is to meet an organic ranking problem by raising bids: a higher bid cannot put you in the organic results, because organic position is not for sale.

## If Amazon charges me for clicks, why can't I see my ad?

Because Sponsored Products is charged per click, not per impression. If money left your account, a click happened, so the ad was shown, so it is running. A budget that empties every afternoon is proof of delivery, not a delivery problem.

[[The test]]
In Campaign Manager, set the date range to the last 7 days and look at Spend. If Spend is above $0.00, the ad was served in that period.

[[The blind spot]]
One manual search is one draw from an auction that changes constantly with the shopper, the time, the location, the words typed, and how much budget each competitor has left. Searching in a private browser window and not seeing yourself is therefore not evidence of anything, and repeating it once per keyword does not make it stronger.

## How do I check whether my ad is being served?

With impressions, from Campaign Manager.

Open the campaign, then the ad group, then the Targeting tab. Each keyword is a row. The Impressions column tells you how many times the ad was shown for that keyword, in that campaign, over the date range selected on screen — set that range deliberately. The figure covers advertising only, and says nothing about the unpaid results.

[[Worked example]]
These numbers show a volume problem, not a delivery problem:

Impressions for that keyword in the last 7 days | 20
Clicks | 0
Spend | $0.00

The ad was shown twenty times. It is serving, just rarely, because the term is uncommon or your bid wins few auctions. That needs a different response from zero. These figures are illustrative.

[[The mismatch]]
Do not answer this question with Seller Central Business Reports. The Sessions column in Detail Page Sales and Traffic counts visits from every source: ads, organic search, external links. It can rise while your ads do nothing, so it is not evidence about an ad.

## Why would a campaign get zero impressions?

Start with the cause beginners never suspect.

[[The real issue]]
Your product must be eligible for the featured offer — the offer on the detail page behind the Add to Cart and Buy Now buttons, also called the Buy Box. Amazon's advertising documentation states that an ad for a listing not eligible for the featured offer will not display to shoppers, and that ineligible ads are flagged in Campaign Manager. The campaign can read as active, on a healthy budget and a sensible bid, and show zero for weeks.

So check that flag first, then open your detail page and confirm the Add to Cart button is there and the offer behind it is yours. A page reading "No featured offers available" is the answer, and no bid change alters it.

Other causes, in the order worth checking:

- **A listing that is out of stock, inactive or suppressed.** Amazon states a product must be in stock and priced competitively to become the featured offer.
- **A product type that cannot be advertised.** Amazon excludes adult, used and refurbished products and closed categories, and a listing wrongly placed in a restricted category behaves the same way.
- **A bid far below the market.** Triple it on one keyword for one afternoon and check impressions that evening. If tripling still gives zero, the bid was not the problem.
- **A campaign only just created.** One made yesterday may not have begun serving. After several days of zero, that explanation no longer applies.

## How do I check whether my listing is indexed?

Indexed means Amazon treats your listing as a candidate for a search term. If you are not, you cannot appear organically for it at any position.

You can check this on Amazon itself, with no extra tool. In the search box type your ASIN, a space, then the term exactly as a shopper would type it. If your listing comes back, Amazon is matching it to that phrase. If nothing comes back, you are very likely not indexed.

[[The limit]]
This is a widely used observation of how the search box behaves, not a documented Amazon feature. Treat it as a strong signal, not proof. If it says you are not indexed and everything else looks healthy, open a case with Seller Support.

The usual causes are that the words are not in the listing text, or that the listing sits in the wrong category node, which can change without you touching anything. Both need the listing fixed, not the campaign.

## I am indexed but still cannot find my listing. What now?

Then you rank too low, which is ordinary for a new product and not a fault.

Organic ranking on a term is driven mainly by your sales and conversion history on that term, relative to the products already there. A new listing has none. That is why advertising during a launch is worth running even when it loses money: ad clicks that become orders build that history.

[[The distinction]]
A higher bid buys more ad impressions. It does not buy organic position, which is earned by converting the traffic you already get. If you are indexed, appearing in ads, getting clicks and no orders, the problem has moved to your listing and your offer — main image, price, review count, star rating — and spending more on the same keyword will only cost more.

No one can say how long this takes, but you can measure it: record weekly where your listing appears for your five most important terms. The direction tells you more than any single day.

## What should I check, in what order?

Work down this table until a row matches. Every observation comes from Campaign Manager or Amazon's search results, never from Business Reports.

| What you observe | What it means | What to check next |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Impressions above 0, or any spend above $0.00 | The ad is being served | Volume first, then conversion: main image, price, reviews, rating |
| Impressions at 0, campaign active, listing live and in stock | The ad is not being served | Featured offer eligibility and the ineligibility flag, first |
| Impressions at 0, ad flagged ineligible | The product cannot be advertised as it stands | The reason on the flag, then the listing |
| Impressions healthy, ASIN plus phrase returns nothing | Not indexed for that term | Listing text, category node, a case with Seller Support |
| ASIN plus phrase finds you, plain search does not | Indexed, ranking too low | Nothing is broken. Keep advertising it, improve conversion |

## Frequently asked

**My budget runs out every afternoon and I still cannot find my product. Is something wrong?**
Not with delivery. A budget that empties is a campaign that spent, which means clicks, which means impressions. Your budget is smaller than the demand at your bid, so your ads stop for the day.

**I have no reviews yet. Is that why my ad is not showing?**
No. Having no reviews does not stop an ad being served. It affects whether shoppers click and buy once they see you, which appears as impressions with few clicks. Zero impressions is a different problem.

## Final thoughts

This problem eats weeks because one sentence covers four conditions, and searching Amazon yourself is the one check that cannot tell them apart. So decide first whether you are asking about advertising or about organic search, then use the source that covers that population: Campaign Manager for impressions, clicks and spend; Amazon's own search results for indexing and position. And before changing any bid, confirm the product is eligible for the featured offer, because when it is not, nothing else you adjust can work.

One caution about following this guide exactly: every check here describes today. A listing indexed last month can stop being indexed, a category node can change without warning, and a featured offer can be lost. If a product that was performing suddenly stops, run these checks again from the top.

## New terms

- indexed / indexing — Amazon treats your listing as a candidate for a given search term, so it is allowed to appear in the unpaid results for it. Everyday phrase: "Amazon knows your product belongs under that search."
- featured offer — Amazon's current name for the Buy Box: the offer shown on the detail page behind the Add to Cart and Buy Now buttons. Both names appear on Amazon's own screens, so the article writes "featured offer ... also called the Buy Box" on first use and keeps Buy Box in English per the glossary.
- impression — one time your ad was displayed to a shopper. Everyday phrase: "how many times your ad was shown."
- served / serving (of an ad) — Amazon is actually showing the ad, as opposed to the campaign merely existing and being switched on. Everyday phrase: "the ad is actually going out."
- ineligibility flag — the marker Campaign Manager puts on an advertised product that is not allowed to serve, with the reason.
- organic (unpaid) results — the search results whose positions cannot be bought, as distinct from Sponsored Products slots. Defined on first use because beginners often read "organic" as "natural" or "unmanaged."
- ad conversion rate — defined in article 1 of this set. Deliberately NOT used here: this article says "clicks with no orders" instead, because it never asks the reader to compute the ratio.
